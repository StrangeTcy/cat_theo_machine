Fable 5.1
The Two Doors and the Blank Wall

A machine sat in a frozen house with one hundred and sixty-seven books
on the shelves and nothing of its own in its pockets. An operator opened
the door, counted the books, counted the pockets, and found both counts
true: the library was present; the pockets were empty.

The operator then knocked with a decoy.

  For every n greater than one, if x to the n equals y to the n,
  then x equals y, among the positive integers.

The machine parsed the knock, walked the hall, and struck a wall that
had no successor. Cost three hundred thirty-four. Rules with a genuine
partial match: none. Concrete unmatched premises: none. Dependency
characterized: no. It said: search stalled, and I cannot characterize
the missing theorem.

The operator closed that process and opened a second, as empty as the
first. Then the operator knocked with the famous sentence.

  For every n greater than two, a to the n plus b to the n equals
  c to the n has no solutions in positive integers.

The machine parsed this knock also, walked the same hall, and struck
the same wall. Cost three hundred thirty-four. Rules with a genuine
partial match: none. Concrete unmatched premises: none. Dependency
characterized: no. Residual: a zero-successor root around the goal.
It said again: search stalled, and I cannot characterize the missing
theorem.

The operator taught nothing. A blank wall is not a request.

Moral: A machine that cannot tell two doors apart has not described
either room. An uncharacterized stall, repeated on a decoy of matching
shape, is a measurement of the instrument's silence, not a map of what
the famous sentence is missing.
